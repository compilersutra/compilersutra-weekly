About

How this weekly works

CompilerSutra Weekly is a curated brief of compiler changes. We explain what changed, why it might matter, and who should care — and we mark uncertainty when the source is thin.

Cadence

Published every Saturday. Each edition covers Saturday→Friday (Asia/Kolkata), frozen at end of Friday. Raw collection stays private; only the curated edition is published. Editions stay at stable URLs under issues/YYYY/YYYY-MM-DD.html.

Publishing lifecycle

Raw data accumulates all week and is never the public newsletter by itself. Pipeline:

  1. Collect (Sat→Fri) — idempotent raw facts into an edition
  2. Freeze (end of Friday) — close the period
  3. Process / review — normalize, cluster, editorial queue
  4. Validate → publish (Saturday) — one immutable curated edition

While the next edition is collecting, the site continues to show the last published edition. Late items after freeze go to the next edition or an explicit addendum — published editions are not silently rewritten.

Sources

Primary: git mirrors of tracked projects (LLVM/Clang, GCC, MLIR paths, selected GPU/AI stacks). Secondary: GitHub releases, selected RFCs/discussions, and compiler-relevant arXiv items when present in the collect window.

Selection and “high signal”

Commits are grouped by related subjects/paths into development themes. Heuristics promote items that look like new support, ABI, codegen, correctness, or multi-commit themes. Noise (reverts, NFC-only, test-only) is demoted. The newsletter is curated from that set — not every collected commit.

We do not invent benchmarks, performance numbers, or completion status. If impact is unclear from the subject/paths, we say so.

Labels

Stage (Frontend / Middle-end / Backend / Runtime & Toolchain), domain (CPU, GPU, AI/ML, …), hardware (when paths/subjects name a target), and audience are assigned independently from evidence. SPIR-V may appear as a hardware/target chip when the change is SPIR-V-specific. “Papers” in the nav is research reading; Explore’s Research domain is the same label used as a filter over stories.
